## 11 September 2026 — hearing as it happens

Measured on the same M1 Pro, 16 GB, macOS 27.0, against the installed profile. Everything below was observed by a script that is checked in; none of it was watched by a person on a live microphone yet.

- **Where the time went.** Timed through the worker's own protocol with a realistic prompt (persona, eight memories, two note excerpts, twelve messages): 3.7–3.9 s to the first token and 4.3–4.5 s to the first sound, against 0.67 s and 1.66 s on the bare prompt earlier figures were taken from. Whisper 0.22 s for a 5 s clip warm, Parakeet 0.11 s warm (its recorded 1.2× was a cold first call), Kokoro 0.29 s a sentence, Smart Turn 30–80 ms an ask. The pipeline spoke 16–19 s for a weather question at "one or two sentences"; the duplex model 10–25 s.
- **The microphone is now heard as a stream.** The native tap converts every buffer to 16 kHz and the model worker's listener thread runs Silero per 32 ms frame, reporting only when speech starts and stops; Smart Turn is asked about the last eight seconds a quarter of a second into a pause and again every 0.4 s; 1.5 s of silence ends a turn regardless. `scripts/verify-turn-detection.ts` streams rendered speech through the real path at real-time pace: speech heard to start 15 ms early and stop 97 ms late against the clip's actual voice, the finished question judged complete at 0.92 in 63 ms, the unfinished thought 0.04, silence never speech (`verification/turn-detection.json`).
- **Endpointing rests on Silero.** Smart Turn only lets a finished thought end sooner. Its check now renders a question five times: every render must clear the authors' 0.5 and the median 0.65; on this Mac 0.653, 0.696, 0.625, 0.805, 0.616 → qualified. The old check phrase ended on a handoff and was heard, rightly, as unfinished; a Kokoro render of a question still sits near the boundary (0.57–0.97 across renders) while real human speech scored 0.84–0.94, so a re-check can fail by luck and the product loses nothing but a second of pause when it does.
- **Interruption has its acoustic proof.** Replies play through the audio engine while the microphone is open, which is the route Apple's echo cancellation subtracts; the engine only starts once the mixer is joined to the voice-processing output at the microphone's rate (left implicit it fails with -10875 on this Mac). `scripts/verify-barge-in.ts` plays an 8.7 s reply out of the speaker with the microphone open: the raw microphone hears 77% of it as speech, the cancelled tap 0% through the engine and 0% outside it (`verification/barge-in.json`). Interruption itself — a person talking over a reply — is still unobserved.
- **The first token comes sooner.** The reasoning model keeps exact prefix snapshots (mlx_vlm's prefix cache); the service worker prepares persona and history between turns, and a turn carries only recall, its state and the words. On a new question: 0.84 s to the first token and 1.7 s to the first sound (0.04 s when the exact prompt repeats). The first piece of speech leaves at the first clause of six words or more. Each role is warmed with a real generation at launch (10.9 s) so the first turn is not the slow one.
- **Replies are shorter, and bounded.** Brief is the default: sixty tokens, one sentence, about six seconds of Kokoro. Measured is 120 tokens. The duplex engine is bounded by seconds of speech per length (8, 14, 45) because it does not hold to a length it is asked for. Saying "shorter" is kept as a procedural memory and drops the length to Brief at once.
- **The model is told what it is.** Every turn carries a state block built from the snapshot: what it hears and whether it can be talked over, what it sees, which models are installed, how much memory it holds, what is connected, and whether the last reply was cut off. Recall now includes earlier exchanges (a transcript index, schema version 3) beside facts and notes, and a first-person message of any length may propose a memory.
- **Checks:** TypeScript passes; the suite passes 45 tests; the native helper builds; the dev application launches, reports "Endpointing is off (silero qualified: true, finishing on meaning: true)" and "replies through the engine: yes", warms four roles, and shuts down on request. The stored settings on this Mac still had every listening switch off at that launch.

- **A spoken turn was 7.8 s and is now 0.74 s to first audio.** Measured on this Mac, warm, question through to the first sound of the answer. The worker held one model at a time, so a single turn loaded four — transcription, recall, reasoning, speech — each evicting the last. Timed against the shipped worker, the eviction cost 2.57 s, 3.17 s and 1.51 s while the work those models actually did came to 0.45 s: **94% of a turn was reloading weights**. Models now stay resident under a 4.5 GB budget, least recently used leaving first, which holds Whisper (459 MB), the embedding model (335 MB), Qwen (2.9 GB) and Kokoro (339 MB) together at a measured 3.90 GB resident and 4.79 GB peak. Transcription moved from Parakeet (2.3 GB) to Whisper, which is smaller and was already measured faster here, 6.9× realtime against 1.7×; Parakeet remains installed and is used if Whisper fails. Nothing is reloaded during a conversation, and the models a turn needs are loaded at launch rather than on the first question. Turn totals fell from 15.65 s cold and 7.83 s warm to 14.48 s cold and 2.19 s warm.
- **A spoken turn can now be held by one speech-to-speech model, and it is faster than the pipeline.** `mlx_audio/sts/` was already inside the Python runtime this application ships — adapters for `lfm_audio`, `nemotron_voicechat`, `moshi` and `mimo_audio` — so "this research profile has no Mac adapter in this worker yet" was true of the worker, not of the runtime. `mlx-community/LFM2.5-Audio-1.5B-4bit` is installed at revision `55d4de65…` (1.7 GB) and **qualified on this Mac**: it heard a Kokoro-rendered "What is the capital of France?" and answered aloud "The capital of France is Paris.", in 17 pieces, at 0.0302 RMS, running at 1.99× realtime once warmed. Through the worker protocol a warm turn reaches **first audio at 0.52 s** against 0.87 s for the pipeline, and speaks 3.04 s of answer in 1.81 s of generation, so once the first piece plays the audio never runs dry. One model at 1.7 GB replaces three at 4 GB.
- The duplex check measures pace twice and says so. The first generation after loading runs at **0.81× realtime** — slower than playback — because MLX compiles its kernels on the first pass; warmed, the same turn runs at 1.99×. A single take would have failed a model that works, so the check warms first, measures second, and records both numbers rather than the flattering one. The application warms at launch for the same reason.
- Handing audio over in pieces is not free, and the check that matters is the seam. Decoding each piece on its own puts a step of **0.130** into the waveform where the model's own largest is **0.047** — an audible click at every join. Decoding each piece with the eight codes before it and discarding the overlap returns it to exactly 0.047. An earlier measurement reporting no seams was wrong: that run generated a single piece, so there were no joins to find.
- **What duplex costs.** Hearing and answering in one pass means there is no transcript of the question before the reply, so memory and notes cannot be searched for the current turn. What was said is transcribed afterwards, off the path to the first word, which keeps it in the conversation record and available to the next turn's recall. Recall therefore lags by one turn on this engine and does not on the pipeline.
- The engine is chosen in Settings → Voice and is gated on what has been observed working: the duplex model must have passed its checks here, and OpenAI Realtime must actually be connected. An engine whose model is removed falls back to the separate local models and never silently to the cloud, which is pinned by a test. **OpenAI Realtime is connectable but no session has ever been established** — verifying the key asks OpenAI for the realtime model itself rather than merely parsing the key, but no API key exists on this Mac, so neither the handshake nor any audio path has been exercised. Local-only mode refuses the connection outright.
- **The reply is spoken as it is written.** The worker synthesizes each sentence the moment it finishes and hands the audio over while the rest is still being generated, because the worker runs one job at a time and a separate request for speech would have queued behind the very generation it was meant to keep pace with. The helper plays the pieces back to back as one utterance, keeping one generation and reporting finished once, so interruption, hands-free resumption and the playback meter all behave as they did. Interrupting speech now also abandons the thought, which it did not need to when the reply was already complete before the first word was spoken. First audio at 0.74 s warm, 2.02 s on the first turn after launch.
- Spoken replies are no longer read out as markdown. A model writing for a screen reaches for asterisks, headings and lists, and a synthesizer either reads them aloud or stumbles; the marks are stripped on both the streaming and the single-piece path, and the model is told its reply will be heard and to answer in one or two sentences. The same question that produced 16.1 s of spoken paragraph now produces 3.0 s of answer.
- A worker reply carrying an error was treated as a success whenever the error text happened to be empty. Python's `StopIteration` stringifies to nothing, so asking the worker to install a model absent from its catalogue answered `{"error": ""}`, which the transport read as falsy and resolved as an ordinary result — the caller received `undefined` and no failure was ever reported. A reply carrying an error key now fails whatever its text says, with "The worker failed without saying why." where there is nothing to quote, and the worker sends the exception's class name rather than an empty string. Found by an install that silently did nothing; pinned by a test that fails without the fix.
- The wake path writes down what it does, because a switch left off and a wake that never fires look identical from outside the application. `runtime.log` now records, at startup, whether the wake word is on at all and whether its model is qualified; every opening and closing of the listening microphone, with whether echo cancellation was granted on that route; the microphone permission when it is what prevents listening, said once rather than every second; and every wake score, which is only taken while somebody is already speaking, so a quiet room writes nothing. A failed wake check is recorded rather than swallowed: one that fails every time was previously indistinguishable from one that never hears the name.
- The microphone can now be held open without recording anything. `listen.start` runs the tap and the rolling history with no file behind it, so `audio.preview` keeps working and nothing reaches disk until a turn actually begins; `listen.stop` is safe when nothing is listening. `audio.start` during a listen **takes the tap over without stopping the engine** and writes the rolling history into the front of the new file, so speech already in the air when a turn begins is not lost — observed on this Mac as the recording growing from 1.38 MB to 3.46 MB with `preRoll: true`. Apple's echo cancellation is requested on the input and output nodes and **reported rather than assumed**: `voiceProcessing: true` here at 48 kHz, and any route that refuses it reports false so that anything depending on it can stay shut. The input format is read after enabling it, because enabling can change the sample rate and channel count.
- `native-audio.json`: 100 native playback-stop trials passed on MacBook Pro Speakers, with p95 10.86 ms at that boundary and varying playback-meter samples. This measures NDJSON request through AVAudioPlayer.stop acknowledgment; it does not measure acoustic barge-in latency.
- The strict design audit reports 44 findings: 43 mistake React Aria `Button` / `onPress` for inert native buttons; one mistakes React Aria `Select` for a native select. The unmodified report is retained in `premium-audit.json`; `design-audit-review.json` records the actual JSX tags and action attributes at all 44 locations. Its automated result remains failed; this is a documented detector limitation, not a clean-audit claim.

## Implemented preview

The only default surface is a draggable 72-point native glass orb. Sphere movement reflects actual activity and playback level; no traveling bar, perimeter beam, or audio ring is rendered. The orb settles when idle and respects Reduce Motion. Settings is the only full window. Text, conversation, context, task evidence, and exact approvals are opened in compact panels.

The preview includes the encrypted task and memory service, explicit local file creation, separate media and credential helpers, pinned private Python runtime, model install/repair controls, per-model checks that record what was observed in the model manifest, a chosen notes folder indexed for recall beside memory, memories the model suggests and the person accepts, pointer and keyboard selection of a screen area, Accessibility enumeration with an approval-bound control press, local speech and reasoning, a hands-free session that reopens the microphone after each spoken reply, selected-window capture with an explicit one-frame-per-second follow mode, scoped recall, routines and repository watchpoints, Codex/Claude adapter code, Apple EventKit connectors, selected Mail metadata/content, and read-only Google OAuth adapters.

## Not qualified or not implemented
- Hearing its name is built, and has never run on a live microphone. The microphone is held open without a file behind it, so nothing reaches disk until a turn begins. Two ways of hearing share it: openWakeWord is asked about short snapshots and never transcribes, and — only if separately switched on — a burst of speech between 0.25 s and 1.2 s is transcribed to check whether it was the bare name, a length gate that is the privacy boundary rather than an optimisation. Interrupting a reply is wired to Apple’s echo cancellation, which the native helper reports rather than assumes, and no acoustic interruption has been measured on any route.

- Codex/Claude paid end-to-end execution has not been run. Codex is reachable: its app server starts, the protocol handshake succeeds, and `account/login/start` returns an `auth.openai.com` address that passes the sign-in allowlist, so connecting is one action in Settings rather than a missing credential. A Codex task no longer requires an Anthropic key; without one the work still runs and the receipt records that no independent review was performed. No Anthropic key is stored, so the review step itself is unexercised. Live Apple/Google connector effects are likewise unexercised. Codex sandbox behavior beyond its documented workspace-write policy needs qualification before distribution.
- Apple Mail sending, Google writes, and full selected-tab content integration are not enabled in this preview.
- Pressing a control is built and gated but never dispatched: it needs the Controls you approve connection, an approval naming the exact control, and a re-resolve immediately before acting, and a resumed task reports the press that already landed rather than repeating it. Nothing has yet pressed a real control, so the dispatch itself and whatever an application does next are unmeasured.
- The visual fallback proposes a place; it never presses one. When a named control has no Accessibility match, the qualified vision model is shown a capture of the application's own controls and returns a point, and the control Accessibility actually finds there is what the approval names. A point that lands on nothing pressable is refused rather than clicked. There is one look, not three: a second look at the same screen returns the same answer, so the person is asked instead. Coordinate-only clicking is deliberately not shipped. Its arithmetic is covered by tests: a point in a captured area maps onto the control that contains it, a point far from every control is refused, and an empty answer is refused. Against real applications it has so far only refused — twice, the proposed point landing outside every control Accessibility knew about — so the refusal is exercised and a correct resolution is not. No press has followed it. Whether the sixty-point radius and the whole-window capture are the right shape is an open question that needs a screen with more than a handful of exposed controls to answer.
- The area selector has not been exercised as an Electron window on a real desktop: whether it takes the keyboard, floats above a full-screen Space, and behaves across several displays are manual checks. A drawn area is a single observation and cannot be followed; only a window can.
- Hands-free conversation is built and gated, and has never run on a live microphone route. It is a session: a qualified microphone the person opens starts one, each spoken reply reopens it once playback has finished, and it ends on an interruption, on a turn finished with nothing said, on a request for work rather than an answer, or on ten seconds of silence, which discards the recording. It rests on automatic endpointing, so switching endpointing off clears it in the same write and a session whose models stop qualifying closes itself. Endpointing in turn cannot be switched on until Silero and Smart Turn both pass their checks on the Mac in use, and Smart Turn does not pass here, so both switches are off on this Mac and Settings now says which model still owes a check instead of answering a press with an error. What has been observed is the decision layer in `src/shared/turn.ts`, under test, and every interface state driven in a browser against the development preview. The reopening itself, acoustic barge-in, and whether a real pause is judged the way rendered speech was are all unmeasured.
- Duplex and experimental models remain disabled pending device-specific qualification. Partial live transcription is not yet implemented; recognition uses finalized recordings.
- A connected notes folder adds to recall; it does not stand in for memory. Both are searched for the same question, memory keeps its own review and correction path, and forgetting the notes leaves memory untouched. A folder joins recall in the personal scope only; a project-scoped conversation does not search it. The first index of a large folder is dominated by embedding and runs in the background, with its stage visible in Settings. Credential redaction is a bounded pattern match, not a guarantee that every secret is caught.
- Scheduled work requires Jarvis to be running. Routines and watchpoints have not completed live long-duration qualification.
- Full-screen Spaces, multiple displays/scales, display removal, sleep/wake, all accessibility settings, 60 fps, combined memory under the standard workload, and 100 acoustic interruption trials per route remain release checks.
- Notarization has not been validated; the packager reports it skipped notarization because no credentials were configured. Developer ID signing and the signed upgrade with its Keychain ACL migration are exercised and recorded above. The packager discovered the existing Developer ID and is configured to sign the bundle. Signature verification and installed launch are recorded separately below.
